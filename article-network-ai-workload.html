AI6 min read

The network is becoming AI’s most demanding customer.

For thirty years the network served people. Now its hardest user is a cluster of GPUs that never sleeps, and it has very different demands.

Enterprise networks were tuned for human-shaped traffic: bursty, latency-tolerant, north-south. AI workloads are the opposite, sustained, latency-allergic, and overwhelmingly east-west. Pointing one at the other exposes every shortcut a decade of “good enough” networking quietly took.

Three demands AI makes that people never did

  • Sustained throughput, not peak. A training run saturates links for hours. Oversubscription ratios that were invisible under human load become the bottleneck.
  • Tail latency is the whole game. Collective operations move at the speed of the slowest flow. A 99th-percentile hiccup stalls the entire job, idling very expensive silicon.
  • East-west by default. The traffic that matters now runs server-to-server inside the fabric, not user-to-datacenter. Topologies designed for the latter starve the former.

You can buy the fastest GPUs on earth and still lose, because the network decided how fast they were allowed to talk.

What changes in the design

Readiness is less about a single product and more about a set of deliberate choices: non-blocking or lightly-oversubscribed leaf-spine fabric; lossless transport tuned for RDMA-style traffic; congestion control that holds up under incast; and observability granular enough to see a single slow flow before it taxes a whole run. Increasingly it also means the network has a control plane intelligent enough to schedule around its own hotspots, AI operating the fabric that AI runs on.

Before you scale a GPU estate, pressure-test the fabric beneath it. The cheapest place to find an AI bottleneck is on a whiteboard; the most expensive is in production, metered by the hour.

The opportunity hiding in the constraint

Treating AI as a first-class network tenant forces a discipline most estates needed anyway: real telemetry, honest capacity planning, automation instead of tribal knowledge. Modernise for the most demanding customer, and every other workload inherits a network that is faster, more observable and easier to run.

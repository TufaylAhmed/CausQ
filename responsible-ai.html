Responsible AI

We help enterprises put AI to work. We do it with guardrails our clients’ risk teams, and ours, can stand behind.

AI only creates durable value when people can trust it. These are the principles we apply to every system we design, build or operate, and that we encourage our clients to adopt as their own.

Our principles

  • Human accountability. A person is always accountable for an AI system’s outcomes. Automation assists decisions; it does not absolve responsibility.
  • Purpose and proportionality. We deploy AI where it genuinely helps, matched to the risk of the use case, not for its own sake.
  • Transparency. Stakeholders should understand where AI is used, what it does, and its limits. We document systems so they can be reasoned about.
  • Fairness. We test for harmful bias and work to mitigate it, and we’re honest about what a system can and cannot do.
  • Privacy and security by design. AI systems are built on the same Zero Trust and data-protection foundations as the rest of the estate.
  • Evaluation before deployment. Systems are measured against clear criteria, and monitored in production, not shipped on a demo and forgotten.
  • Human oversight. For consequential decisions, a person stays in the loop with the ability to review, override and intervene.

The goal isn’t AI that’s merely powerful. It’s AI that’s powerful and governable.

How we put it into practice

For client engagements, we build governance in from the first diagram: defined use-case risk tiers, evaluation harnesses, guardrails, audit logging, and clear escalation paths. For AI that operates infrastructure, we add conservative defaults, staged rollout and the ability to fall back to known-good states. We’d rather ship something dependable than something flashy.

Our own use of AI

We hold ourselves to the same standard internally, using AI to do better work, with human review of anything that affects clients, and with care for the confidentiality of the information entrusted to us.

This page sets out principles, not contractual commitments. Specific obligations for an engagement are defined in the relevant statement of work.

Zero Trust isn’t a product. It’s a way of running the network.

You can’t buy Zero Trust. You can only operate it, and most failed programs failed because they tried to install it instead.

Zero Trust gets sold as a SKU and bought as a project, which is exactly why so many initiatives stall. The principle is simple, never trust, always verify, assume breach. The hard part is that it’s a change to how the network is run, every day, not a box that goes in a rack.

The shift in one sentence

Old model: get inside the perimeter, and you’re trusted. New model: every request, user, device, workload, is authenticated, authorised and continuously evaluated against context, no matter where it originates. The perimeter doesn’t disappear; it moves to identity.

The flat trusted network is the single most exploited design decision in enterprise IT. Zero Trust is the discipline of never building one again.

Why programs stall, and how to keep yours moving

  • Boiling the ocean. Teams try to segment everything at once. Start with one crown-jewel application and one user population; prove the pattern; then expand.
  • Identity debt. Zero Trust is only as good as your identity data. Clean up accounts, devices and entitlements first, it’s unglamorous and decisive.
  • Friction backlash. If verification annoys people, they’ll route around it. Tune for risk-based, mostly-invisible checks so security buys trust instead of spending it.
  • No telemetry. “Assume breach” is meaningless without the visibility to detect one. Instrument before you enforce.

A useful test: can you name, for your most sensitive system, exactly which identities can reach it, from which devices, under what conditions, and prove it changed the moment someone left? If not, that’s your first sprint.

Running it, not installing it

Operationalised well, Zero Trust becomes invisible plumbing: policy expressed as code, access granted just-in-time, posture evaluated continuously, and every decision logged. It pairs naturally with SASE for access and with modern segmentation in the fabric, but the technology is downstream of the operating model. Get the model right and the products become interchangeable. Get it wrong and no product saves you.

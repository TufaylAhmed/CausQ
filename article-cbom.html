Building a cryptographic bill of materials, the practical way.

Every post-quantum plan begins with the same unglamorous step: finding out where your cryptography actually lives. Almost no one knows.

A cryptographic bill of materials, a CBOM, is a living inventory of every algorithm, key, certificate, protocol and library your organisation relies on, and the data each one protects. It is the map you need before you can migrate anything. It is also the artefact most enterprises discover they’ve never had.

Why you can’t skip it

Post-quantum migration, certificate hygiene, incident response, regulatory attestation, all of them assume you know where cryptography is used. Without a CBOM, “migrate to PQC” is a wish, not a plan, because you can’t scope, sequence or prove it. The hidden, hard-coded, vendor-buried cryptography is exactly the cryptography that will bite you.

The most dangerous algorithm in your estate is the one you forgot you were running.

A practical way to build one

  1. Pick the lenses, not the boil-the-ocean. Inventory from several angles, network (what’s negotiated on the wire), code and dependencies (libraries and calls), certificates and key stores, and vendor attestations, rather than chasing a single perfect source.
  2. Automate discovery. Use traffic analysis, dependency scanning and certificate inventory tooling to find the bulk; reserve humans for the long tail and the judgement calls.
  3. Capture context, not just crypto. For each finding, record what data it protects and how long that data stays sensitive. That’s what turns a list into a priority order.
  4. Make it live. A CBOM that’s a one-time PDF is obsolete the next deploy. Wire it into CI/CD and asset management so it updates itself.

Aim for “good and current” over “perfect and stale.” A 90%-complete CBOM that refreshes automatically beats a 100% snapshot that’s wrong within a month.

What it unlocks

Once the CBOM exists, the hard questions become answerable: which systems hold our longest-lived secrets? where are we still using deprecated algorithms? if a library breaks tomorrow, what’s exposed? It becomes the backbone of crypto-agility, the foundation that lets you change cryptography as routine maintenance instead of a fire drill. Start it now; it’s the cheapest, highest-leverage move in the whole quantum-readiness journey.

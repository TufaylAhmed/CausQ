Harvest now, decrypt later: the breach you won’t see for a decade.

The most consequential data breach of the 2030s is being committed right now , quietly, against encrypted traffic nobody can read yet.

Somewhere, an adversary is copying your encrypted data and filing it away. They can’t read it today. They’re betting that within a decade a cryptographically relevant quantum computer will let them read all of it, retroactively. That bet has a name: harvest now, decrypt later.

It is the rare cyber-risk that is both certain in direction and uncertain in timing. We don’t know the exact year RSA-2048 falls. We do know that the data you encrypt today, medical records, financial positions, state secrets, intellectual property, will still be sensitive when it does. For anything with a long shelf life, the clock has already started.

Why a single “migration” is the wrong mental model

Most organisations frame the quantum threat as a future project: one day we’ll swap our algorithms. That framing fails for two reasons. First, you cannot migrate what you cannot see, and almost nobody has a complete inventory of where cryptography lives in their estate. Second, cryptographic standards will keep moving. The goal is not to arrive at a new fixed algorithm; it is to become crypto-agile: able to change cryptography the way you change a TLS certificate, routinely, centrally, without a re-architecture each time.

Crypto-agility is the difference between a one-off panic and an operating capability.

What to actually do this quarter

You don’t need a quantum computer to start. You need visibility and a plan. In order of value:

  • Discover. Build a cryptographic bill of materials (CBOM): every key, certificate, algorithm, library and protocol in use, and the data each one protects.
  • Prioritise by shelf life. Rank systems by how long their data stays sensitive. Long-lived secrets move first, they’re the ones being harvested today.
  • Pilot hybrid PQC. Deploy NIST-standardised post-quantum algorithms in hybrid mode (classical + PQC together) where the stakes are highest, starting with your own perimeter.
  • Centralise crypto. Pull hard-coded cryptography behind services and policy so the next change is a configuration, not a project.

The deadline isn’t the quantum computer. It’s the moment your longest-lived secret becomes readable. Subtract your data’s sensitivity lifetime from the day Q-Day arrives, that’s when you needed to be done.

The strategic reframe

Handled well, post-quantum readiness is not a tax. The same discovery that maps your cryptography also maps your risk; the same agility that defends against quantum also shrinks the blast radius of every future algorithm break and key compromise. You are not buying insurance against one exotic event, you are building a muscle you will use for the next twenty years.

The companies that move early will do it calmly, in daylight, on their own timeline. The ones that wait will do it under a regulator’s deadline or an incident’s glare. We’d rather help you do the former.
